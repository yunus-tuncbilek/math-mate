**Lecture 1: Partial Fractions**
When integrating rational functions, decompose the fraction into simpler parts called partial fractions. Each term should have a denominator that is a factor of the original one. For linear factors, use constants; for irreducible quadratics, use linear numerators. After decomposing, integrate term by term. Example:
(5x - 1) / ((x - 1)(x + 2)) = A / (x - 1) + B / (x + 2).
Solve for A and B, then integrate.

---

**Lecture 2: Series**
A series Σ a_n converges if its partial sums approach a finite limit. Key tests:

* p-Series: Σ (1 / n^p) converges for p > 1.
* Comparison Test: Compare with a known convergent or divergent series.
* Alternating Series Test: If terms alternate in sign, decrease in magnitude, and approach zero, the series converges.

---

**Lecture 3: Derivatives**
To differentiate complex functions, use the rules systematically:

* Product Rule: (fg)' = f'g + fg'
* Quotient Rule: (f/g)' = (f'g - fg') / g²
* Chain Rule: For composite functions, multiply the derivative of the outer function by that of the inner function.
  Practice with trigonometric, exponential, and nested functions like f(x) = tan(sin(x² + 1)) to strengthen fluency.
